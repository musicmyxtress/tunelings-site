Tunelings

How breeding works

The same guide is in the game: Menu, How breeding works.

Two copies of everything

Every Tuneling carries two copies of every gene, one from each parent. When two Tunelings breed, the baby gets one copy from each parent, picked at random, like a coin toss. That is how real inheritance works, and it is why brothers and sisters can sound so different.

Copies are passed on whole. They are never mixed or watered down on the way, so a copy can hide for generations and then show up again.

How two copies decide what you hear and see

Depending on the trait, the two copies work together in one of these ways:

  • One copy wins and the other hides. Most traits work like this: call type, voice, wings, antennae and more. A triple caller might secretly carry a stutter copy.
  • Rare traits need two copies. Harmony, echo and glow only show when both copies carry them. Two ordinary parents that each carry one copy can surprise you.
  • Some pairs make something new. One rising copy and one falling copy give a call that rises and falls; one tall copy and one squat copy give a round body.
  • Some pairs both show. One growl copy and one trill copy give a growling trill.
  • Some traits blend. Pitch, speed, fur colour and fluffiness show the middle of the two copies. The copies themselves still pass on whole, so a middle-pitched Tuneling can have a very high baby.

New copies

Now and then a copy changes as it is passed on (a mutation), so brand-new traits can appear. Wild Tunelings, rewards and lures bring in copies your flock has never had.

How close is this to real genetics?

The heart of it is real: two copies, one passed on at random from each parent, dominant and hidden copies, pairs that combine, mutation, and chance. These are the rules Gregor Mendel found with pea plants.

It is simplified for play. Each trait is one gene (in real animals, things like colour come from many genes together), every gene is passed on separately, there are no male and female Tunelings, and mutations happen far more often than in nature, so you get to see them.

Why a trait you are chasing can vanish

This surprises people, but it is real genetics at work, not bad luck in the game:

  • A Tuneling can show a trait and still carry a different, hidden copy. Breed two triple callers that both secretly carry stutter, and about one baby in four stutters.
  • A rare trait thins out when it meets ordinary mates. Your echo singer has two echo copies, but its baby with an ordinary mate gets only one, so the baby sounds ordinary. Breed that baby with another ordinary Tuneling and each grandchild has only an even chance of even carrying echo. Within a couple of generations it can quietly disappear.
  • Blended traits drift back towards the middle. A very high singer's pitch might come from just one of its copies. If the baby gets the other one, it comes out lower. Real breeders call this regression to the mean.
  • Small flocks lose copies by chance. An unlucky run of coin tosses can lose a copy for good, and selling or sending out a hidden carrier takes its copies with it.

Tips: chasing a rare trait like echo

Echo, harmony and glow need two copies, so think in copies, not in what you can hear.

  • Find the carriers first. Listen closely (3 notes) on a Tuneling to hear what it carries but doesn't show. A carrier of one echo copy sounds completely ordinary.
  • Breed carrier with carrier and about one baby in four has echo. Breed an echo singer with a carrier and about half do. Two echo singers always have echo babies.
  • Breed babies back to the parent with the trait. If your only echo singer had an ordinary-sounding baby, that baby carries one echo copy: breed it back to the echo singer for an even chance of echo.
  • Don't sell or send out a carrier while you are chasing its trait. That copy may be one of the only ones you have.
  • Take rewards that carry a hidden gene. When a visitor says a creature carries a hidden echo gene, that is a free copy.
  • Lure it in. A lure for echo, harmony or glow (15 notes) brings a Tuneling that already shows it, which means it has two copies.
  • Echo has to be released in the market first. Until then, no Tuneling can carry it.

Tips: everything else

  • To make a trait reliable, find Tunelings that carry two copies of it. If Listen closely finds nothing hidden for that trait, both copies match, and every baby gets that copy from that parent.
  • To push pitch or speed further, breed the two Tunelings closest to the end you want, again and again. Each generation edges a little further, and the occasional mutation helps.
  • Keep a song of anything precious on your song shelf. Hatching an egg from it brings back a Tuneling with the very same genes, even in a new game: a safety net if a trait disappears. It uses your egg for that game, and the song leaves the shelf when it hatches.
  • Grow your nest when you can. Room means you can keep carriers instead of choosing between them.
  • If a request looks impossible right now, skip it early. Skipping costs 1 note at first and 1 more for every birth the visitor has waited.
  • Not sure what a trait sounds like? The Sound guide in the Menu plays each one on the same plain call, so you can compare them.
